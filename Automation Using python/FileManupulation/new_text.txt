In Python, managing files requires careful handling to ensure they are
opened and closed properly. Leaving files open can lead to data corruption
or other unexpected behaviors. The with statement simplifies this process,
making file operations cleaner and more efficient. When you use with to
open a file, it ensures that the file is closed automatically once the operations
within its block are completed, regardless of whether these operations were
successful or raised an exception. This automatic handling not only makes
the code more readable by eliminating the need for an explicit close() call
but also adds a layer of safety, ensuring that resources are promptly freed
and reducing the risk of file-related errors.
